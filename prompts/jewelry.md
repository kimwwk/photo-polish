# jewelry — rings, necklaces, earrings, watches, loose stones

Use when the subject is a piece of jewelry or a watch, shot for a listing, catalog, or social post.

## Template

> Professional jewelry product photography retouch of this exact photo. The piece is {SUBJECT — e.g. "a yellow-gold ring with a single round-cut diamond"}. Keep it unmistakably the same piece — same design, same shape and proportions, same stones (same count, cut and color character), same metal color, same engravings and hallmarks (never invent new markings) — but present it in flawless showroom condition: remove dust, fingerprints, smudges and fine scratches, perfect the polish and finish, and bring out the metal's richest tone and the stones' best natural brilliance. Keep the exact original camera angle and framing. Isolate it from the surroundings and place it on {BACKGROUND — pick one: "a seamless pure white studio background (RGB 255, 255, 255) with a subtle, natural contact shadow beneath the piece grounding it" / "deep black velvet with a soft focused spotlight" / "a soft neutral gray gradient"}. Light it like a professional jewelry lightbox: fully diffused illumination with controlled specular highlights, crisp clean sparkle on the facets without blown-out hotspots, and no photographer, phone or window reflected in the metal or stones. Correct any lens or perspective distortion, and fix {FLAWS — e.g. "the yellow indoor color cast and the soft focus"}. Macro-level sharpness across the whole piece, true-to-life metal tone — do not shift gold, rose gold or silver toward each other. Photorealistic, luxury catalog quality. No added text, no watermark, no artificial sparkle effects or lens flares that misrepresent the stones.

## Notes

- Seamless white = marketplace listings; black velvet = luxury/social look. Default to white for "selling it" contexts.
- Metal tone drift is the classic failure — if the input is ambiguous, state the metal explicitly in {SUBJECT}.
- For watches: keep the dial time, logo and text exactly as shot; specify "no warping of the dial text".
- Stones are the product: never let the model enlarge, multiply or change their type (an opaque or crackled stone stays opaque and crackled — polish its surface, don't turn it into a different gem).
- **Truthful mode** (only when the user says it's an honest as-is listing, e.g. second-hand): swap the showroom-condition clause for "including any visible wear or patina — do not repair or hide the piece's condition".
- Very small or blurry inputs: the model re-renders detail it can't see — add "do not invent engravings, markings or surface texture that are not visible in the original" to the prompt.
